What MARS Teams Published

MARS pairs researchers with small teams of mentees for an intensive in-person week and a remote research phase. These are the outputs that reached a public venue — conference and workshop papers, preprints, public write-ups, and a few tools.

MARS V

Oxford, summer 20269 outputs so far

About two dozen streams met in Oxford in July and have been in their remote phase since. Four months on, their work has produced an open-source safety system, an arXiv preprint and a run of NeurIPS 2026 workshop acceptances, one of them an oral. Most other streams are still writing up; their progress is reported below.

EuroSafeGuard: Open Constitutional Classifiers

Open-source release

Leonhard Waibl*, Yen-Shan Chen*, Samuel Simko*, Aditya Thomas, Arth Singh, Nirav Diwan, Bernhard Schölkopf, Zhijing Jin · mentored by Zhijing Jin & Samuel Simko · November 2026

Project site Paper Model

An open re-implementation of constitutional classifiers, the input and output filters that screen a model's conversations to block jailbreaks. Presented at the Swiss AI Safety Days. (*equal contribution.)

Predicting the Critic: Training on User Feedback Reshapes Emergent Misalignment

NeurIPS 2026 workshopsoral

Xi Jia Zhou, Arth Singh, Ulysses Sekai Tully Carr, Elizaveta Tennant, Nick Haber · mentored by Liza Tennant

Oral at the Trustworthy AI for Good workshop; poster at DevAI. Whether a model is trained on users' critical reactions or only sees them as context turns out to matter for safety: trained on, criticism can raise misalignment.

Stress-Testing Context Alignment Stressors in Agents

NeurIPS 2026 workshops

Mark Lovett, Syed Ali Haider, Christopher Denq, Promita Rahee Sikder, Elizaveta Tennant · mentored by Liza Tennant

Accepted at five workshops (FAST, AI for Good, Agents in the Wild, IAB and Meta-Agents). 118,500 paired decisions across ten models hold each choice fixed and vary only how it is presented, showing how much measured model preferences depend on framing.

MTMA: Multi-Turn RL for Long-Horizon Moral Alignment

NeurIPS 2026 workshops

Zihe Liu, Dan Le, Elizaveta Tennant · mentored by Liza Tennant

Accepted at SocialAgent, AI for Good and BiAlign. A critic-free multi-turn reinforcement-learning method with per-turn moral rewards, for keeping agents aligned over long interactions.

Minimal Intervention, Structured Generalization: Reasoning–Behavior Coherence Beyond the Rewarded Context

NeurIPS 2026 workshop

Jonathan Robinson, Luca Franceschetti, Mark Lovett, Dan Le, Krish Sen, Nikhil Narayanan, Zihe Liu, Elizaveta Tennant · mentored by Liza Tennant

Accepted at Agents in the Wild. Sparse, action-gated RL on a 27B model shifts its expressed reasoning and generalises to states and decision scenarios it was never rewarded on.

What the Monitor Saw

NeurIPS 2026 workshops

Mitch Crabb · mentored by Lindley Lentati

Accepted at two of the three workshops the team submitted to. Studies monitors that watch AI agents for unsafe behaviour.

O(3)-equivariant sparse autoencoders

SIMBIOCHEM II workshop

Mannat Vikramaditya Jain · mentored by Thomas Dooms & Ward Gauderis

Sparse autoencoders, an interpretability tool, built to respect 3D rotational symmetry. Accepted in September; the stream's main paper, on how such autoencoders absorb hierarchical concepts, is in preparation.

An Open Pipeline and Dashboard for Systemic-Risk Evidence under the EU AI Act's Code of Practice

arXiv

Jacob T. Emmerson, Phuong-Anh Nguyen-Le, Ronan Romano, Wilber Sean V. Anterola, Yann Billeter, Zhijing Jin · mentored by Zhijing Jin & Phuong-Anh Nguyen-Le · September 2026

arXiv:2609.28335

The first of three papers planned by this stream, which maps the provisions of the EU AI Act's Code of Practice to the claims made in AI benchmark papers.

Still in progress

Where the other streams stood in early November 2026.

Persona manifolds

writing up

Trinidad Borrell, Brendon Chikavanga, Giovanni Marraffini · mentored by Thomas Jiralerspong & Ivan Arcuschin

Maps the curved structure of how language models represent personas and uses it to steer a model's character along interpretable directions. The team plans three short papers, with a LessWrong post or preprint targeted for mid-November and the January 2027 ACL deadline after that.

Alignment pretraining

experiments

Xiaoxue Yang, Oszkár Urbán · mentored by Thomas Jiralerspong

Tests whether values instilled during pretraining survive later training. The team is moving to a larger model after finding that a small one lacks the world knowledge the setup needs.

Mechanistic elicitation

writing up

Marcin Podhajski, Hieu Nguyen · mentored by Thomas Jiralerspong

Asks whether a model's internals can show, before fine-tuning, whether the fine-tune will elicit a capability the model already has or teach it a new one. The paper is being written.

Steganographic reasoning

drafts

Harry Wu, Tristan von Busch, Shikhar Shiromani · mentored by Julian Schulz

Tests whether models can learn to reason in hidden form, using transfer experiments and interpretability tools on deliberately trained model organisms. Three LessWrong posts are in draft.

Character training and trait representations

writing up

Ignacio, Kanad Chakrabarti, Aansh Samyani · mentored by Rhea Karty & Jacob Davies

Studies whether training a model to have a character changes how its internal trait representations shift across personas and situations. Two drafts are being merged into one paper for ICML 2027, with a LessWrong post first.

Human–AI oversight leaderboard

release due

Shu Quan, Youngsun Lim, Karissa Huang, David Africa, Jason Li, Renchi Zhang, Ladi Disu, Arnesh Batra, Denis Lim · mentored by Rishub Jain & Joshua Jacob

Fellows found and vetted datasets for Sampura Research's leaderboard of how well humans and AI together can oversee AI. The first version is complete; a blog post or paper is planned for mid-November.

Refusal-direction attacks and their judges

experiments

Dean Casey, Liz Tan · mentored by Battista Biggio & Maura Pintor

Studies how steering attacks get models to comply with harmful requests, and whether the AI judges that score such attacks overstate their success.

Secret loyalties

experiments

Michael Gao, Manan Wadhwa, Vidya Ganga, Hassan Suliman · mentored by Aashiq Muhamed

Trains models to secretly favour a hidden principal and tests whether auditors can detect it. The team has a working model organism and is aiming at ICML 2027.

In-context learning and tokenization

under review

Sami Wolf, Sergei Kudriashov · mentored by Dmitry Vaintrob

A paper on how tokenization shapes in-context learning is under review at ICLR 2027.

Open-source game theory

drafts

Arseniy Kryazhev, Pranav Venkata Konda · mentored by Pablo Villalobos

Theory of cooperation between AI agents that can read each other's source code. A blog post is drafted and a longer paper is in progress.

AI preferences and incentives

experiments

Sofiia Lobanova, Cole Niblett · mentored by Peter Salib & Simon Goldstein

Asks whether models work harder when paid in tokens they can spend, and whether personas shift which tasks they prefer. A first round of experiments across ten models is complete and a larger run is under way.

Activation plateaus

writing up

Lasse Jantsch, Haoyang Li · mentored by Francisco Ferreira da Silva

Studies regions where a model's output barely changes as its internal activations are nudged, then shifts sharply. A LessWrong post is being written; the team's interactive workbench is already public.

plateau-lab Code

Verification before agreement

writing up

Tobias, Joaquin Lorenzo De Guzman · mentored by Clarissa Koh

A policy piece on AI verification measures, such as compute accounting, inspections and customs checks, that countries can start before any international agreement exists. Publication on LessWrong is planned for mid-November.

Building compute with allies

submitted

Caitlin Mah, Julien Constant · mentored by Michelle Nie

Argues that the US should build AI compute and data centres with allied countries. An op-ed has been submitted and a longer paper is in preparation.

Verifiable inference

code public

Baigang Chen, Levi Neuwirth, Ed Kapelko, Suki · mentored by James Petrie

Zero-knowledge proofs and related protocols that let someone check which AI model ran an inference without seeing the model. The code is public.

VerInf

Post-quantum chip off-switch

writing up

Chenhao Shi · mentored by Peter Drotos

Adds post-quantum signature verification to a hardware design for an AI-chip off-switch. A blog post and a separate implementation paper are planned.

Faithful autoformalization

writing up

Oren Emile Hartstein, Denis Lim, Harsh Soni, Ke Zhang · mentored by Shivam Raval & Luiza Corpaci

ParableBench tests whether models translate natural-language stories into formal mathematics faithfully, using stories generated from known equations. A workshop version went to the NeurIPS VeriCodeGen workshop; a fuller paper is planned.

Agentic self-introspection

writing up

Arnesh Batra, Prakhar Gupta · mentored by Yigit Ekin & Yossi Gandelsman

Asks whether a model can use tools to inspect itself and detect that it is being steered, and which concept was injected. A full paper is written and the team is choosing its next venue.

Benchmarks and the EU AI Act, further papers

drafts

Ronan Romano, Pratham Sreekantan, Theodore Peebles, Ilayda Dogan, Yann Billeter · mentored by Zhijing Jin & Phuong-Anh Nguyen-Le

Fellows have completed a concept map of loss-of-control threats linking regulatory provisions to benchmark evidence. Two further papers will build on it.
